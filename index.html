My Story

Systems shaped me. Now I help people navigate and change them.

I've spent my life moving between worlds. From Ludhiana to Bengaluru, and then across an ocean to Canada, each transition asked me to learn a new set of unwritten rules - to figure out which parts of myself were welcome in which room, and which weren't. That experience didn't just shape who I am. It made me a better practitioner.

"I bring lived knowledge of what it feels like to be an outsider navigating unfamiliar systems - and I channel that into helping people find footing in spaces that were never designed with them in mind."

With a Master of Science in Psychology and frontline experience at WorkBC, Kundan Vidya Mandir, and Mantra4Change, my career has spanned educational equity work, psycho-educational support in schools, and disability case management in the Canadian workforce system. Across all of it, my approach stays the same: meet people where they are, not where the intake form expects them to be.

I'm a neuroaffirming practitioner - genuinely, not just as a line on a resume. That means I don't frame disability as something to be corrected. I frame it as one dimension of a full human life, deserving of thoughtful, individualized, and culturally-grounded support. Disability justice frameworks that centre intersectionality: because race, gender, language, immigration status, and disability are never separate from one another in a person's real life.
